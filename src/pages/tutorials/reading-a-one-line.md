> **Learning note:** A simplified one-line omits detail. Always consult current approved drawings, site procedures, and qualified personnel before any real-world work.

## Start with the drawing information

Check the drawing title, revision, date, legend, and stated scope. Confirm that the diagram is the current document for the system you intend to understand.

## Trace the path

Follow the electrical path from the source toward the loads. Identify how the drawing represents major buses, transformers, feeders, breakers, disconnectors, and loads. Use the legend rather than relying on symbol memory.

## Note the ratings and boundaries

Look for voltage levels, equipment ratings, instrument transformers, protective devices, and normally open points. Make a list of information that is missing or requires confirmation from another drawing or data source.

## Keep interpretation separate from fact

Record what the diagram explicitly states separately from what you infer. A one-line is useful for understanding relationships, but it does not replace detailed schematics, switching instructions, or an approved safety process.
